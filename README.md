## What it does

A timer that sits **on top of every other window** — above your browser, above
your editor, above whatever is distracting you. No tab to hunt for, no window to
switch to.

- **Focus sessions.** Pick a length, press start, watch the ring drain.
- **Breaks that run themselves.** Four focus sessions, short breaks between,
  then a long one. It moves you along automatically if you want it to.
- **Your tasks.** Add what you're putting off, give it an icon and a colour, and
  the timer shows what you're supposed to be doing.
- **Today.** Minutes focused, per task, plus a streak for consecutive days.
- **Zen mode.** Shrinks to a small translucent countdown that stays out of your
  way.
- **It gets out of the way.** Closing it tucks it into your system tray and the
  timer keeps running.

## Using it

| Button | What it does |
|:---:|---|
| ⤢ | Open the full panel — tasks, timer length, breaks, sounds |
| ◐ | Zen mode — shrink to a small translucent countdown |
| 📌 | Keep it above every other window |
| ☾ ☀ | Dark or light |
| — | Minimize |
| ✕ | Tuck into the tray — **the timer keeps running** |

Drag it anywhere by the top bar or the ring. Double-click the big digits to
start or pause.

**Your first session:** hit **⤢**, type in the thing you're avoiding, set your
minutes, collapse it back with **⤡**, and press **▶ Start**.

---

## Your data

Everything stays on your computer — tasks, settings, history, streak. There is
no account, no sign-in, no sync, and the app never makes a single network
request. Nothing you type goes anywhere.

---

## Good to know

- **Closing with ✕ doesn't quit it.** It hides in the system tray and keeps
  counting. Right-click the tray icon to bring it back or quit properly.
- **`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`F` starts and pauses from any app** — but not on Linux
  desktops running Wayland, which blocks apps from claiming shortcuts. Open the
  full panel and the **Shortcut** row tells you which you have.
